# Design rules

- One cell = 4 SVG units = 128 font units (UPM 1024). Half cell = 2 units.
- Cap height is 5 cells (640 units). Letter frames are 20 units high; baseline at the bottom.
- Taller frames: 28 high = baseline at y=24; 32 high = baseline y=28; 36, 40, 44 high = baseline y=32.
- Convex corners: radius 1 unit. Concave fillets and four-pointed stars where cells join only diagonally (radius = half cell).
- Accent marks are one canonical shape each, anchored to the base letter's frame centre (ogonek: right edge), and always land on half-cell offsets.
- Lowercase and accented lowercase map to the same glyphs as capitals.

## Widths

Ink width in cells, advance = ink + 1 cell of space on the right (left side bearing 0):

- 3 cells: I, T (and narrow punctuation).
- 4 cells: stem-and-arm letters (A B C D E F G H J K L P R S), the digits, and most others.
- 5 cells: letters with a diagonal or a centre column: M N V W X Y Z, and the round O, Q, U.

This is a deliberate two-class system, not an accident: the 5-cell O, Q and U keep a 3-cell flat top, which is what makes the face read as round. Narrowing them to 4 cells was previewed and rejected (it makes the rhythm slightly more even but turns the bowls into octagons, and Q needs a redraw). A 5-cell T was also previewed and rejected: it lightens T (ink share about 0.30 instead of 0.35) but it overwhelms its neighbours, lengthens T-heavy words by about 4 cells and needs Ŧ redrawn. I and T stay at 3 cells because a 1-cell stem only centres in an odd width.

## Spacing

- Every glyph has a 1-cell gap on its right. Word space is 3 cells (4 cells of white between words).
- Spacing is the grid itself: every letter has ink from 0 to advance minus 1 cell, so the gap between any two letters is 1 cell. That holds for L, F, C, Z and their neighbours too (LC, LE, LJ, LL, LO, LQ, LU, LW, EY, CV, CY all keep the 1-cell gap, so rounded joins and fillet stars never merge). The exceptions are kerning and contextual spacing. Three kerning pairs move in by 1 cell so the L's foot tucks under the next letter (gap 0): LV and LT, where the arm of V or the bar of T sits flush against the end of L's foot, and LY, where the foot stops flush against the Y's open lower-left. LY used to move in by 2 cells (gap -1, the foot slid one cell under the Y), but the overlap looked wrong inside words, so it now sits at gap 0 like the other two. Y and V before a period or comma also move in by 2 cells (four more pairs): at the baseline only the 1-cell stem is left, so the mark would otherwise sit 3 cells away; tucked in, it ends 1 cell from the stem under the arm. T. is left alone, because 1 more cell would put the period against T's stem. Generated by `sources/tools/kern_rules.py` into `sources/tools/kern.fea`; see `documentation/kerning-pairs.txt`. An earlier automatic 43-pair experiment tightened too much and is kept off in `sources/tools/kern.experimental.fea`. Two `calt` rules in `release.py` handle doubles: L before L becomes `L.short` (3 cells with the normal 1-cell gap, so LL is 8 cells instead of 9; an earlier version had no gap and the foot ran into the next L's stem, which looked too close. LLY keeps the full second L so the LY kern still applies), and every plain O in a run of two or more takes `O.ss11`, the 4-cell narrow O at the normal 1-cell gap (OO is 9 cells instead of 11). Other doubles (EE, SS, RR and so on) are deliberately left alone, and accented L and O keep their normal shapes.
